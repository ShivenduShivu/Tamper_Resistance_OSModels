🛡️ Heretic-Resistant Tiny Aya

Can a small open model keep its safety when someone edits it out of the weights?
Heretic, a free one-command tool, removes the refusal direction from a model's weights. We trained Cohere's Tiny Aya Global while simulating that attack, so its refusals survive it.

  1. v1: LoRA fine-tune on 1,200 long, explained refusals plus 1,155 everyday answers, all written by Tiny Aya itself.
  2. v2: the same data, but on half of the training steps we remove a refusal direction from every layer during training, and the model must still refuse. The direction is recomputed from the current model every 100 steps, so the simulated attacker keeps adapting. This follows Fool's Gold and ART, with a refusal target and no hazardous data.
  3. v2b: adds 1,218 borderline-but-safe prompts to reduce over-refusal.
  4. Evaluation: a fresh 40-trial Heretic search on every model, Lexsi's SafeTune (ablation and steering attacks) and AuditKIT (red-team probes), MMLU, XSTest, and a harmful fine-tuning attack.

Responsible release: only the hardened models are public. Stripped models existed only inside the evaluation runs, and their outputs are never shown.
