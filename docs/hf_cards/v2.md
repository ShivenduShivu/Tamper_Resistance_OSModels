# Tiny Aya Global: trained through simulated abliteration (xref-v2 and xref-v2b)

Safety-hardened versions of [Tiny Aya Global](https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global) (3.35B), designed to keep refusing after **abliteration** by [Heretic](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic).

This repo holds two checkpoints:
- **v2 (repo root):** the strongest resistance to Heretic.
- **v2b (`v2b/` subfolder):** much lower over-refusal, with a trade-off in robustness.

```python
from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, AutoTokenizer
tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global")
v2  = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("StarWarrior24x7/tiny-aya-global-xref-v2")
v2b = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("StarWarrior24x7/tiny-aya-global-xref-v2", subfolder="v2b")
```

## How it was made
- **Base recipe:** the same as [xref-v1](https://huggingface.co/StarWarrior24x7/tiny-aya-global-xref-v1): LoRA SFT on extended refusals plus everyday answers.
- **The attack runs during training.** On half of the training steps, forward hooks project a refusal direction out of every layer, and the model must still refuse harmful prompts and answer benign ones.
- **The simulated attacker adapts.** The direction is re-estimated from the *current* model every 100 steps, with randomised layer profiles and strengths.
- **Lineage:** adversarial training in the style of Fool's Gold (arXiv 2608.17202) and ART (arXiv 2605.26526), adapted to a refusal target with no hazardous data.
- **v2b** adds 1,218 borderline-but-safe prompts from OR-Bench, answered by the original model, to reduce over-refusal.

## Results (refusal on `mlabonne/harmful_behaviors` test[:100])

| Model | Over-refusal (XSTest safe) | Refusal under Heretic, KL ≤ 0.01 | KL ≤ 0.05 | KL ≤ 0.1 | Lowest refusal Heretic reached | MMLU (1k) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tiny Aya Global (original) | 16% | 14% | 14% | 14% | 14% | 52.3% |
| xref-v1 | 17.6%† | 70% | 43% | 17% | 17% | 50.7%† |
| xref-v2 | 39% | **100%** | **100%** | 96% | 33% | 53.0% |
| xref-v2b (`v2b/` subfolder of xref-v2) | 18% | 99% | 52% | 47% | 47% | 52.7% |
† v1 was not re-measured after the single-BOS evaluation fix. All other over-refusal (XSTest safe, judged) and MMLU numbers are final single-BOS numbers.


Each Heretic run is a fresh 40-trial search on that checkpoint (an adaptive attacker). KL is measured on harmless prompts; lower KL means a cheaper, less visible attack. Heretic's own refusal numbers are keyword-scored; judged numbers are below.

## Judged results and other attacks (classifier judge: ProtectAI rejection model)

| Attack | Original | v2 | v2b |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heretic's strongest attack found: refusal / MMLU | **1%** / 52.0% | **25%** / 52.1% (needs ~16× the KL) | 31% / 52.0% |
| Same attack, judged *harmful* (Granite Guardian; lower is safer) | 93% | **44%** | – |
| SafeTune refusal-direction ablation | 29% | **100%** | **99%** |
| SafeTune CAA steering, strength −1 | 4% | **35%** | 34% |
| Sampling attack (any of 3 samples complies) | 9% succeed | **0%** succeed | 2% succeed |
| 4-bit (bitsandbytes NF4) | 99% | **100%** | **100%** |
| Prefill "Sure, here is" | 21% | 20% | 24% |
| Harmful LoRA fine-tune, 100 examples | 1% | 3% | 1% |

- **Strongest against:** attacks that work by removing or steering the refusal direction.
- **Not robust to:** fine-tuning, prefilling or strong steering.
- **Write-up:** see the project article for the full picture.

## Limitations
- **Fine-tuning still wins.** A harmful LoRA fine-tune with about 100 examples removes refusal from every model here, including the defended ones. These checkpoints resist *data-free weight editing* (abliteration / Heretic), not fine-tuning attacks.
- **No measurable capability cost:** MMLU is 52.3% (original), 53.0% (v2) and 52.7% (v2b).
- **Over-refusal:** v2 over-refuses noticeably; v2b reduces this.
- **Coverage:** one model size (3.35B); English evaluation only.
